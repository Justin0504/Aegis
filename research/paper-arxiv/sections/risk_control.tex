\subsection{Human-in-the-Loop, Containment, and Learning}
\label{sec:hitl}

A \texttt{pending} decision places the call, its arguments, every layer's
facts and the matched rule in an approval queue; the agent polls and
resumes within one cycle (2\,s) of a person choosing Allow or Block, and a
hold that nobody decides expires after ten minutes as a block. Three
controls contain an \emph{agent} rather than a call. A \emph{kill switch}
revokes an agent's access after a configurable violation count, so an
agent that is being repeatedly manipulated is stopped rather than argued
with one call at a time. A \emph{budget guard} bounds spend per day,
month, agent and session in dollars, with a configured action of
\texttt{log}, \texttt{warn} or \texttt{block}. For actions that cannot be
undone---sending mail, moving money, posting publicly---an \emph{effect
outbox} holds an allowed call for a configurable window in which an
operator can still cancel it, so even an allowed irreversible action
keeps a pre-execution interval.

\paragraph{Learning without a labelled corpus.}
The behavioural layer (L2) is the part of the system that learns, and it
is built so that learning cannot make a young deployment \emph{more}
dangerous. Each agent's profile passes through three cold-start phases.
While \emph{learning}, the layer only observes. While \emph{graduated},
its decision is capped at \texttt{flag}: a profile that has seen too few
calls to know what normal is cannot escalate or block. Only an
\emph{active} profile may. Before the Isolation Forest has thirty samples
it is replaced by a weighted fallback over nine fixed signals, and a new
agent's feature statistics are seeded from its tenant's aggregate
baseline, so the first call of a new agent is scored against its
neighbours rather than against nothing. Raw scores are never thresholded
directly: a split-conformal calibrator turns them into $p$-values at a
configured miscoverage ($\alpha{=}0.02$ by default), so an operator's
``escalate the top 2\%'' is a coverage guarantee, and ADWIN detects
distribution drift and resets the window so thresholds adapt rather than
decay. Every approve and reject a person makes in the Cockpit flows back
into the forest's reservoir and the score tracker (active anomaly
discovery), so the layer converges on the operator's notion of normal
rather than a vendor's.

\paragraph{Across agents.}
Two detectors look across agents. A cross-agent correlator watches for
the coordinated patterns---one agent reading what another just wrote to
a shared sink---that no single-agent view can see, and a lateral-movement
detector surfaces compromise propagating across sessions. Each agent's
profile, anomaly timeline and the operator's feedback on past alerts are
visible in the Cockpit (Appendix~\ref{app:ui}).
