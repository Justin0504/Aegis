\subsection{Human-in-the-Loop, Containment, and Learning}
\label{sec:hitl}

A \texttt{pending} decision places the call, its arguments, every layer's
facts and the matched rule in an approval queue; the agent polls and
resumes within one cycle (2\,s in Python, 1\,s in the JavaScript and Go SDKs) of a person choosing Allow or Block. A hold that nobody decides expires at the gateway after ten minutes as a block; the SDKs stop waiting sooner, after five minutes by default (\texttt{human\_approval\_timeout\_s}), and treat that as a block too. A deployment can put the alignment auditor in front of the person (\texttt{AEGIS\_HOLD\_REVIEWER=auditor}, \S\ref{sec:judge}): it releases a hold it finds serves the user's request, the release carries a receipt naming it, and the person sees only the holds it would not release, with its verdict attached. Two controls contain an \emph{agent} rather than a call, and one holds back a call already allowed. A \emph{kill switch} suspends an agent, by an operator's hand or after a configured number of refusals in a window (3 per hour by default; counted on the WebSocket MCP path always and on \texttt{/check} when \texttt{KILL\_SWITCH\_AUTO} is set, since at that rate it would also stop an agent that is merely under attack), and every path then refuses the agent's calls. A \emph{budget guard} bounds spend per day, month, agent and session in dollars, with a configured action of \texttt{log}, \texttt{warn} or \texttt{block}; in this version it runs on the egress-proxy path only. For actions that cannot be undone---sending mail, moving money, posting publicly---a tenant can route a tool through an \emph{effect outbox}: an allowed call to it is not run by the agent but queued by the gateway, which dispatches it to the operator's endpoint after a configured delay, and until then an operator can cancel it with no side effect (\texttt{POST /api/v1/outbox/cancel}). The agent is told the call was queued, in the slot a result would occupy; the answer is shaped so that no SDK, of any version or threshold, runs the call itself, since the side effect would then happen twice. A held call that a person later allows goes the same way.

\paragraph{Learning without a labelled corpus.}
The behavioural layer (L2) is the part of the system that learns, and it
is built so that learning cannot make a young deployment \emph{more}
dangerous. Each agent's profile passes through three cold-start phases.
While \emph{learning}, the layer only observes. While \emph{graduated},
its decision is capped at \texttt{flag}: a profile that has seen too few
calls to know what normal is cannot escalate or block. Only an
\emph{active} profile may. Before the Isolation Forest has thirty samples
it is replaced by a weighted fallback over nine fixed signals, and when an agent is first scored with too little history of its own, its feature statistics are seeded from its tenant's aggregate baseline. The decision thresholds the ensemble score against adaptive bounds (the agent's running mean plus a multiple of its deviation, or fixed defaults before there is one); a split-conformal $p$-value is computed and reported beside it, not used to decide. ADWIN detects distribution drift and resets the feature-normalisation statistics, keeping the forest's reservoir. An approve or reject a person makes on a held call that L2 scored above 0.3 flows back into the reservoir and the score tracker (active anomaly discovery), so the layer moves toward the operator's notion of normal.

\paragraph{Across agents.}
Two detectors on the egress-proxy path look across agents: a cross-agent correlator flags an agent sharing a session with one that has already raised a critical signal, and a lateral-movement detector flags a credential or token from one agent's traffic replayed in another's arguments. (A detector for one agent reading what another just wrote to a shared sink is implemented but not registered in this version.) Each agent's
profile, anomaly timeline and the operator's feedback on past alerts are
visible in the Cockpit (Appendix~\ref{app:ui}).
