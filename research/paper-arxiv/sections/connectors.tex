\subsection{Connectors}
\label{sec:connectors}

\system is meant to sit inside an existing stack rather than replace
one, so most of its surface is connectors (Table~\ref{tab:connectors}).

Two design rules hold across the table. First, a connector is
configuration, not code: a tenant adds a Splunk sink or a PagerDuty
destination by writing a config row, and the gateway picks it up on the
next event without a release. Second, nothing outbound is on the
decision path: sinks, alerts and exports are fire-and-forget with their
own queues and dead-letter handling, so a slow SIEM cannot slow an agent.
The gateway runs as one Node.js service with SQLite or PostgreSQL behind
it, and ships with Docker Compose, a Helm chart and one-click
configurations for the common hosts.
